ServiceNow Change & Release Management Interview Q&A

Risk Assessment • Risk Calculation • Approval • CAB • Scheduling • Deployment • Validation • Rollback

End-to-End Release Workflow

Application Change Ready → Testing / QA / UAT → ServiceNow Change → Implementation / Validation / Rollback → Risk Assessment → Risk Calculation → Risk Level → Approval → CAB if applicable → Scheduling → Go / No-Go → CI/CD Deployment → Production Validation → Closure / Rollback
Interview focus: The process is about controlled and traceable production delivery. ServiceNow manages change governance, risk and approvals; the CI/CD platform normally performs the actual deployment.

1. Risk Assessment

1. What is Risk Assessment in ServiceNow?
Risk Assessment is used to evaluate the risk of a proposed change. The requester or change team answers configured questions about the change, such as impact, complexity, testing, dependencies and rollback capability. ServiceNow evaluates the responses and produces an assessment result.
2. What type of questions are asked during Risk Assessment?
The exact questions depend on the organization's configuration. Typical areas include business impact, affected applications or CIs, number of users affected, service outage, complexity, testing, dependencies, previous change history and rollback capability.
3. Give examples of Risk Assessment questions.
  • Does the change affect a business-critical application?
  • How many users or services could be affected?
  • Will there be an outage or service degradation?
  • Has the change been fully tested?
  • Are application or infrastructure dependencies involved?
  • Is a documented rollback plan available?
  • How difficult would it be to roll back?
  • Has this type of change been successfully performed before?
  • Are there other changes scheduled for the same system or window?
The actual questionnaire is organization-specific.
4. Who completes the Risk Assessment?
The team creating or submitting the change provides the information needed for the assessment. Depending on the organization's process, this may be the application team, release team or DevOps team. The answers should accurately represent the actual change.
5. How do Risk Assessment answers affect the change?
The answers contribute to the risk evaluation. Higher-impact, more complex or harder-to-recover changes can produce a higher risk result. The resulting risk level can influence the required approval and review process.
6. What if the application team says the change is Low Risk but ServiceNow calculates High Risk?
Review the Risk Assessment responses and identify which factors caused the higher result. If an answer is incorrect, correct it with appropriate justification. If the assessment is accurate, follow the higher-risk approval path rather than lowering the risk simply to proceed.
7. How do testing and rollback affect risk?
Complete testing and a clear, proven rollback approach reduce implementation and recovery risk. Incomplete testing, difficult rollback, unknown dependencies or significant production impact can increase risk. The exact scoring depends on the configured assessment.

2. Risk Calculation

8. What is Risk Calculation?
Risk Calculation determines change risk using configured conditions or rules. Factors can include change type, impact, affected CI, timing and other organization-specific conditions.
9. What is the difference between Risk Assessment and Risk Calculation?
Risk Assessment is questionnaire-based: the requester answers configured questions about the change. Risk Calculation uses configured rules or conditions to determine risk. An organization can configure either or both mechanisms.
10. What happens if Risk Assessment says Moderate but Risk Calculation says High?
Follow the organization's configured ServiceNow behavior. Where both mechanisms are used and the higher risk is selected, the change is treated as High Risk and follows the corresponding approval process.
11. Can risk rules be customized?
Yes. ServiceNow change-risk behavior is configurable. Organizations can define assessment questions, scoring, conditions and thresholds to match their change-management policies.

3. Risk Level and Approval

12. What do Low, Moderate and High Risk mean?
They represent the level of risk associated with implementing the change. Low-risk changes generally have limited and predictable impact. Moderate and High-risk changes have progressively greater potential impact, complexity or recovery risk. Exact thresholds are organization-specific.
13. How does risk affect approval?
Risk helps determine the level of review and approval required. Higher-risk changes may require additional approvals or CAB review, while lower-risk changes may follow a simpler workflow. The exact path is determined by organizational policy and ServiceNow configuration.
14. Can production deployment happen before required approvals?
No. Required approvals should be completed before production implementation. The purpose of change management is to ensure the production change has been reviewed and authorized before deployment.
15. What happens if an approver rejects the change?
The reason for rejection is reviewed. The implementation, testing, risk, impact or rollback information is updated as needed, and the change is resubmitted through the required approval process. The rejection should not be bypassed.

4. CAB

16. What is CAB?
CAB stands for Change Advisory Board. It reviews planned changes from business, technical, risk and impact perspectives. Typical review areas include implementation, testing, dependencies, timing, risk and rollback.
17. Does every production change go through CAB?
No. It depends on the organization's policy, change type and risk. Standard changes may be pre-authorized. Normal changes follow the configured approval process and may require CAB. Emergency changes follow an expedited process.
18. What does CAB review for a high-risk change?
CAB reviews what is changing, why it is needed, affected applications or CIs, business and technical impact, risk, testing completed, implementation plan, dependencies, maintenance window, validation plan and rollback plan.
19. What happens if CAB rejects a change?
The reason for rejection is addressed. Additional testing, risk mitigation, implementation changes or a stronger rollback plan may be required. The change is then updated and resubmitted for the required approval.
20. Does CAB perform the deployment?
Normally, no. CAB provides governance and approval. The actual production deployment is normally performed by the DevOps, release or deployment team through the approved CI/CD process.

5. Scheduling and Go / No-Go

21. What is a change window?
A change window is the approved period during which the production implementation is scheduled. Before the window, approvals, deployment artifacts, implementation steps, rollback, dependencies and team availability should be confirmed.
22. What is conflict detection?
Conflict detection helps identify overlapping or potentially conflicting changes affecting the same application, CI or environment. Conflicts are reviewed before proceeding so that competing changes do not introduce additional risk.
23. What is checked before production go/no-go?
  • Required approvals are complete.
  • Risk assessment is complete.
  • Testing and UAT are complete.
  • Production artifact/version is confirmed.
  • Implementation and rollback plans are ready.
  • Dependencies are understood.
  • Change window is confirmed.
  • No unresolved conflicts exist.
  • Required teams are available.
  • Validation and monitoring steps are ready.
If a critical prerequisite is missing, the release should not proceed.
24. What if the application team is not ready during the approved window?
The deployment should not be forced simply because the window was approved. Readiness is reassessed, stakeholders are informed, and the change can be rescheduled or updated according to the organization's process.

6. Production Deployment

25. How does ServiceNow connect with Jenkins or another CI/CD tool?
ServiceNow provides the change-management, approval and audit record, while Jenkins or another CI/CD platform performs the deployment. Integration can associate the deployment with the ServiceNow change so the approved change, application version, pipeline execution and production result are traceable.
26. Who performs the production deployment?
The DevOps, release or deployment team normally executes the production deployment through the approved CI/CD pipeline. ServiceNow manages the change record and governance; it does not necessarily perform the deployment itself.
27. How can only approved changes be allowed into production?
The process combines ServiceNow approvals with CI/CD controls. The change must have the required approvals before the production stage is allowed. Where integration exists, the pipeline can validate the change information or approval status before deployment.
28. What information should be captured about the deployment?
The change should be traceable to the application version or artifact, deployment time, pipeline/build, implementation result, validation result, issues encountered and final outcome.

7. Production Validation

29. What is validated after production deployment?
Application health, deployment status, logs, monitoring, connectivity, health checks and key application functions are validated. Depending on the application, smoke tests or API checks may also be performed.
30. What if the pipeline says SUCCESS but the application is not working?
Pipeline success does not necessarily mean application success. Application logs, infrastructure or Kubernetes status where applicable, monitoring, health checks and recent configuration changes are reviewed. If the issue cannot be safely stabilized and rollback criteria are met, the approved rollback plan is followed.
31. When is a production change considered successful?
The change is considered successful when the deployment completes, the application passes the agreed validation or smoke tests, monitoring shows healthy behavior, and the expected business functionality is confirmed.

8. Rollback and Failed Changes

32. What is a rollback plan?
A rollback plan explains how to return the application or environment to the previous stable state if the deployment fails. It should identify what needs to be reverted, how it will be performed, who is responsible and how recovery will be validated.
33. When should a rollback be initiated?
Rollback should be based on predefined criteria, such as failed health checks, critical functionality failure, significant user impact or an issue that cannot be stabilized within the agreed window. The decision should follow the approved recovery plan.
34. What happens if a production deployment fails?
The impact is assessed first. If rollback is required, the documented rollback plan is executed, recovery is validated and monitoring continues. The ServiceNow change is updated with the actual outcome. If the failure creates a production incident, the incident-management process is also followed.
35. What if rollback also fails?
The incident is escalated to the appropriate application, DevOps, infrastructure and support teams. The priority becomes restoring service. Additional remediation or recovery procedures are followed, stakeholders are kept informed, and the change and incident records are updated.

9. Senior-Level Scenarios

36. A change is classified as High Risk, but the application team wants to deploy tonight. What happens?
The high-risk classification is reviewed to confirm that the assessment is accurate. If the risk is valid, the required high-risk approval and CAB process must be completed before deployment. The release should not bypass governance because of a deadline.
37. The application team completed testing but has no rollback plan. Should the release proceed?
For a significant production change, the recovery approach should be defined before proceeding. Testing confirms expected behavior, while the rollback plan defines how to recover if production behavior differs from expectations. The change should be reviewed and the rollback approach documented before approval.
38. CAB approved the change, but the implementation plan changes just before deployment. What happens?
The change is checked to determine whether the modification is within the approved scope. If it materially changes risk, impact, implementation, timing or rollback, the change should be reviewed and re-approved according to the organization's process rather than assuming the original CAB approval covers it.
39. A developer says, “It is just a small configuration change; no ServiceNow change is needed.” What is the response?
First determine whether the change qualifies as a standard or pre-authorized change. If the organization's policy requires a ServiceNow record, the production change should not bypass the process simply because the technical change is small.
40. A production deployment causes an incident. What happens?
The immediate priority is service restoration. The impact is assessed and rollback or remediation is performed as appropriate. Monitoring and communication continue throughout recovery. The ServiceNow change and incident records are updated with deployment details, impact, recovery actions and final outcome.
41. How is the release made auditable?
Traceability is maintained from the application change through testing, ServiceNow change, risk assessment, approvals, deployment artifact, CI/CD execution, production validation and final closure. This provides evidence of what was changed, who approved it, when it was deployed and what the result was.
42. How should an end-to-end production release process be explained?
The application change first completes development, testing and UAT. A ServiceNow change is then created with the implementation plan, impact, risk, validation plan and rollback plan. Risk Assessment is completed and the configured risk calculation is evaluated. The resulting risk level determines the required approval path, including CAB when applicable. Once approved and scheduled, the production deployment is executed through the approved CI/CD pipeline. Post-deployment validation, smoke testing and monitoring confirm application health. A successful change is documented and closed; a failed change follows the rollback or remediation process and is documented appropriately.
